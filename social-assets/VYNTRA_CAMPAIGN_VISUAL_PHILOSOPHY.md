# Signal Geometry

Signal Geometry treats flow as a visible structure. A line changes direction when a system changes state: opening, accumulation, transition, resolution. Space stays open so the eye can read the path before it reaches a label.

White acts as a field with its own weight. Deep violet carries structure and concentration; cyan marks movement. Gradients belong inside a small number of forms, and light follows material instead of decoration. Every color transition receives painstaking attention.

Scale sets the rhythm. One central form carries the composition while fine ticks, small circles, and quiet reference marks reward a closer look. Repetition should feel measured, with enough variation to show that a person placed each element by hand.

Composition balances certainty with room to explore. Strong alignment gives the image a clear spine; a single offset or open edge keeps it from feeling mechanical. Typography stays sparse and becomes part of the geometry, never a paragraph placed over it.

The final surface should feel meticulously crafted and labored over with care. Edges, spacing, color, and visual rhythm need master-level execution. Each mark should appear deliberate, the product of deep expertise and patient refinement.
